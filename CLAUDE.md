# CLAUDE.md

The Admin Panel is the web app Shor's staff use to administer the Discord server: a Discord login for the staff, blocked words kept in a Discord AutoMod rule (ADR 0002), and an audit log (ADR 0004). It is a Next.js app (App Router) in TypeScript under `src/app/`, to be hosted on Vercel's free Hobby plan, on a `vercel.app` domain with no domain bought for it (Gilles, 2026-10-24, while grilling #51; the Hobby plan still waits on « Does Shor make money? » in `docs/agents/open-questions.md`), from this repository, public since 2026-10-27 so that Hobby can deploy it (ADR 0007, #100), that talks to Discord's REST API only (ADR 0003). As of 2026-10-28 it serves, to the staff alone, through the Discord login of `src/staff/` and its pages under `/login` (#10), under a header that names the member, beside their avatar (#140), and links each section, `/blocked-words`, to which `/` redirects (#71), where they list, add and remove the blocked words of the panel's AutoMod rule (#11), and `/audit-log`, where they read who changed that rule, or timed out, kicked or banned an account or lifted its timeout or ban, and when, from Discord's audit log, 50 changes a page, each beside the avatar of the member who made it, a sanction with its reason and, for those who moderate, a link to its account's record (#12, #141, #182), and, to those who also hold a moderation role, `/records`, where they find an account by the start of its name, by its ID or by its mention, and open its record, `/records/{userId}`, with its names, dates, roles and standing on the Discord server (#176), where they time out a member for one of seven durations, or lift their timeout, with a reason that Discord's audit log keeps in the format of `src/audit-log/`, and a direct message to the member, through `src/sanctions/`, which refuses beforehand what the role hierarchy forbids (#179), where they kick a member, with a reason and a direct message sent before the kick (#180), and where they read, under every record, the history of the account's sanctions and their lifting that Discord's audit log keeps, given through the panel, in Discord or by AutoMod (#177), in the « parchemin et cuir » palette of the shadcn/ui theme, light or dark as the system is set (#70, #132, **Interface**); `src/discord/` holds the client of Discord's REST API, which the login and the staff check call, its in-memory fake and the HTTP adapter that answers for Discord over a fake (#59, #63); `src/monitoring/`, which `src/instrumentation.ts` calls, sends panel errors to a Discord webhook (#78); the repo also holds the docs and the tooling, Playwright's end-to-end setup included (#63).

## Language

Three things are in **French**: chat with the user, GitHub issues and their board (title, body, comments, and the board's descriptions and view names; see `docs/agents/issue-tracker.md`), and the panel's interface, which is French only, with no translation layer. Everything else is in **English**: docs, `GLOSSARY.md`, ADRs, PR text and PR comments, commit messages, and every word written into the codebase: file names, identifiers, logs. URLs count as file names, since each segment is a folder under `src/app/`: `/blocked-words`, not `/mots-interdits`.

## No comments

The repository holds no comments, in any file that supports them: code, tests, scripts, CSS, HTML, SQL, YAML, `.env.example`, whose variables are documented in the README. Two exceptions only: a shebang, and a tool directive the code needs to work (`eslint-disable…`, `@ts-expect-error`, `/// <reference …>`, `@vite-ignore`, `webpackIgnore`), in the forms **Directives** allows.

What a comment would say goes elsewhere: the why of a change in its commit message, a decision in an ADR, a term in `GLOSSARY.md`, an invariant in a test and its name. Code copied from elsewhere, such as skymp-heavy-rp or EsportCards, loses its comments on the way in.

`pnpm check:comments` checks every tracked or untracked, non-ignored file. A hook runs it before `gh pr create`, and CI on every PR and every push to `main`.

## Facts and docs

- **Facts apart from inferences.** Everything you write to docs, tickets or reports keeps verified facts, with their source (`file:line`, commit, URL and date, command output), visibly separate from inferences.
- **Challenge with evidence.** When the code, the docs, `GLOSSARY.md`, an ADR and the user's request disagree, name the contradiction, quote both sides, and ask which one wins. Record the answer through domain-modeling.
- **Unrecoverable why.** When nothing explains a decision or a fact the work needs, append it to `docs/agents/open-questions.md`: the question, where it shows up, who is likely to know, what is blocked on it.
- **Docs as you go.** Docs are updated the moment they become true, in the same change. `GLOSSARY.md` is a glossary and nothing else; ADRs follow domain-modeling's bar. When docs contradict the code, fix them in the same change if it takes a few lines, otherwise list them in your report.
- **Scope.** Each diff stays inside its ticket. Problems you spot nearby are loose ends: groom-backlog files them as tickets.
- **Gotchas.** When a session hits a trap the environment doesn't reveal, add one line under **Gotchas**, as for a warning silenced until something changes (**Reasons**). Delete lines that stop being true.

## Costs

No third party the panel relies on may ever send a bill or take a hidden fee, as Gilles decided on 2026-10-27, calling it a golden rule, as #9 was about to open the Vercel account (#123). It covers every service, account and tool the panel, its CI and its tooling use, today's and any to come. [`docs/costs.md`](docs/costs.md) says, for each, what the panel uses, why it cannot bill, what would make it bill, and what it can cost otherwise, with its sources.

- **No way to pay.** Every account the panel relies on stays on a free plan with no payment method on file, so that a limit reached blocks or pauses the service instead of billing it, as Vercel's Hobby and GitHub Free do (`docs/costs.md`). So nobody adds a payment method, starts a trial, upgrades a plan, or buys a domain, credits or an add-on, nor installs an integration or app that bills, on an account the panel relies on, not even for a free first month: a card added for one purchase may stay on file (inferred from one unofficial report, `docs/costs.md`, **Vercel**).
- **A paid plan** would reopen this rule: should the panel need one, as #13 foresees if Shor turns out to be commercial (`docs/agents/open-questions.md`), Gilles decides it in an ADR before anything is signed.
- **A new tool.** Before a new third party comes in, whether a service, an account, an integration, an app, or a package, an action or a registry that reaches a hosted service when it installs or runs, its entry goes into `docs/costs.md`, from its own pricing and terms: whether it asks for a payment method, what happens at each limit the panel could reach, and what would make it bill. One that needs a card before it can be used, or that bills past its free quota even without one, stays out. A package that only runs locally needs no entry, since the npm registry has its own (**Adding a dependency**). Neon, the store ADR 0004 picks after the MVP, gets its entry before its account opens, from the store's grilling (#175).
- **Agents** create no account, type no payment method, accept no trial or upgrade, buy nothing, and install no integration or app that bills, by any means: a terminal, a dashboard, an MCP server. Before Gilles takes such a step, they tell him what it costs, with its source, and they stop at a screen that asks for a card or offers a trial in place of the free plan.
- **Rechecked every month.** Free plans change: the monthly session of **Dependencies** reads again the pages `docs/costs.md` cites, and an entry whose facts moved is updated before anything else, or its service leaves (#97).

## Architecture

How a feature is written end to end, as Gilles decided while grilling #47 on 2026-10-23, then #49, #54, #52 and #50 for the lines that point to **Errors**, **Testing**, **Interface** and **Monitoring**. It was written before the code: each ticket that writes the code follows it.

- **Folders.** `src/app/` only routes: its `page.tsx`, `layout.tsx` and `route.ts` call a domain folder named after a `GLOSSARY.md` term, such as `src/staff/` (login, session, staff role), `src/blocked-words/` or `src/audit-log/`. A domain folder holds its logic, its components, its `actions.ts` and their tests. `src/discord/` is the adapter to Discord's REST API, `src/ui/` holds what the interface shares (**Interface**), and `src/monitoring/` the logs and the alerts (**Monitoring**). The error pages are the one exception (**Errors**).
- **The Discord seam.** `src/discord/` exposes two interfaces, one method per endpoint the panel calls but the alerts' webhook (**Monitoring**), typed with `discord-api-types`. `DiscordApi` uses the panel's application token and is bound at construction to one Discord server, so no method takes its ID: AutoMod rules, audit log, reading a member, searching the members, reading a user, a ban, the bans and the roles (#176), then the Discord server itself, for its owner and its roles, the application's own user, read from `/users/@me` with that token, modifying a member, for a timeout, opening a DM channel and sending a message in it (#179), and removing a member, for a kick (#180). Each returns the fields of `discord-api-types`' object that the panel uses, picked from it, where the method's result is bigger than the panel needs, such as `Guild` or `Message` (#179). It also exposes that server's `guildId`, a property rather than a method, from which `memberAvatarUrl`, in `src/discord/member-avatar-url.ts`, builds the URL of a member's avatar (**Avatars**, #140), as `userAvatarUrl` does an account's Discord avatar (#176). `DiscordOAuth` uses the client ID and secret: authorize URL, code exchange, current user. The real implementations call `fetch`, with neither `@discordjs/rest` nor `@discordjs/core`. `src/discord/fake-discord.ts` keeps a Discord server's state in memory and, like Discord, adds an audit log entry on every write. Only tests import it: `pnpm dev` runs against the panel's development application, "Shor Panel Dev", and Gilles' test Discord server (#7). A `no-restricted-imports` rule of `eslint.config.mjs` refuses it and its HTTP adapter (**Testing**) anywhere in `src/` outside `*.test.*` files.
- **Entry points only wire.** Pages, layouts, `actions.ts`, Route Handlers, `proxy.ts` and `instrumentation.ts` hold no logic of their own and never read `process.env`. Those that need Discord get the real clients from `createDiscordApi()` and `createDiscordOAuth()` and pass them on, as in `addBlockedWord(discord, staffMember, word)`; tests pass the fake instead, with no `vi.mock`. The login callback's Route Handler and `proxy.ts` pass `createMonitor()`'s `Monitor` the same way (**Monitoring**). The callback's Route Handler passes `createDiscordApi` and `createDiscordOAuth` themselves, which its domain function calls inside its `try`, so that a missing variable is a panel error it reports too, since the callback throws nothing (**Error pages**; Gilles, 2026-10-26, on #10). Only `src/discord/` reads Discord's variables, `src/staff/` the staff's, and `src/monitoring/` its own (**Security**, **Variables**). Vitest cannot render `async` Server Components (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/testing/vitest.md:9`), so what a page does lives in functions tested directly.
- **The staff check.** Every page but the login pages under `/login` and the error pages, and every Server Action that writes, starts with `requireStaffMember` from `src/staff/`, and every domain write takes the branded `StaffMember` it returns (ADR 0005). The pages under `/records` start with `requireModerationAccess` instead, which runs `requireStaffMember`, then calls `notFound()` for a staff member who holds no role of `MODERATION_ROLE_IDS`, so that they see the not-found page rather than `/login/refused` (ADR 0005, #176), and so does the Server Action that changes a sanction, whose domain functions also throw for a `StaffMember` who does not moderate (#179, #180). Like the staff check's redirects, it runs in the part of the page under `<Suspense>`, so the page's static shell shows first, and the response keeps its `200` (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/04-functions/not-found.md:193`). Until #61 writes `not-found.tsx`, the not-found page is Next.js's own, in English. `requireStaffMember` never returns a failure: it redirects to `/login` without a readable session, and to `/login/refused` a member who is not on the Discord server or lacks the staff role. The login callback, which has no session yet, runs the same check by Discord user ID and redirects itself (ADR 0005). It uses `redirect` rather than `forbidden()`, which needs `experimental.authInterrupts` (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/04-functions/forbidden.md:17`) and gives a Route Handler an empty body (`node_modules/next/dist/server/route-modules/app-route/module.js:259-263`).
- **Writes** are Server Actions, in their domain folder's `actions.ts`, marked `"use server"`, and end with `refresh()` once the write lands. Logging out is a Server Action too, which only ends the session, then sends the member to `/login/logged-out`, a static page that says so, with « Se connecter » (Gilles, 2026-10-26, on #10). Route Handlers serve OAuth2 only: `/login` and `/login/callback`.
- **Validation** uses zod 4 throughout the repo, config included. Domain functions validate what comes from the browser, such as an action's `FormData` or the callback's query string. An invalid URL parameter counts as absent. Discord's responses are typed, not validated.
- **Components** are Server Components. `"use client"` goes only on a leaf that needs browser state or events, in its domain folder, on some components shadcn/ui's CLI copies (**Interface**), and on the two error pages Next.js requires it of (**Errors**); never on a page, a layout or anything that reaches `src/discord/`. A client leaf may hold browser state, such as `useActionState`'s, but no logic: it hands that state to a component whose output follows from its props alone (**Testing**).
- **Caching.** Nothing from Discord and nothing from the session goes through `use cache`, since the panel reads Discord back on every view (ADR 0002). Only the static shell prerenders; the part of a page that waits on Discord sits under `<Suspense>`, with a French fallback. That part, and a Route Handler, await `connection()` before they create a client: the build prerenders a page, and a `GET` Route Handler, until it reads the request (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/01-getting-started/15-route-handlers.md:89`, `:124`), and a client created there parses variables that a build outside Vercel lacks, which failed `pnpm build` on `/` and `/login/callback` (2026-10-26). It also keeps `/login` from prerendering one `state` for every visitor. `/records/[userId]` exports `instant = false`: its `userId` is unknown at build, so the header's `usePathname` suspends outside `<Suspense>` there (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/04-functions/use-pathname.md:75`), which failed `pnpm build`; the page then renders at each request, where nothing outside `<Suspense>` waits on Discord (2026-10-28, #176).
- **`proxy.ts`** sends a request without a readable session to `/login`, on every route but `/login`, everything under it, and static files: the callback opens no session for a member without the staff role, who would otherwise loop from `/login/refused` back through Discord. It authorizes nothing.
- **Imports** that leave their folder use `@/*`, mapped to `src/*`; imports within a folder stay relative, except in `src/ui/` (**Interface**). Vitest resolves the alias only with `resolve.tsconfigPaths: true`, which Vite 8.3.5 leaves off by default.

## Errors

How the panel fails, as Gilles decided while grilling #49 on 2026-10-23, then #53 for what a successful write returns. Like **Architecture**, it was written before the code.

- **Four kinds of failure.** _Refused input_: the staff entered something the panel refuses, or the Discord server is in a state they can fix in Discord, such as already holding 6 keyword rules. _Refused access_: no readable session, or not staff (**The staff check**). _Discord unavailable_: a 429 the panel does not retry or that comes back, a 5xx, a network error or a timeout. _Panel error_: everything else, a misconfiguration included, such as a revoked token or a missing permission; the staff can only report it.
- **`DiscordError`** is the one class every method of `DiscordApi` and `DiscordOAuth` throws, network errors and timeouts included. It carries the HTTP status, Discord's JSON `code` and `message`, the delay of a 429, the method and the route's pattern, as Discord's docs write it, such as `/guilds/{guild.id}/members/{user.id}`, never the path filled in, since Next.js prints its properties in the logs (**Monitoring**), and says whether Discord is unavailable. One absence is an answer rather than an error: `getMember` returns `null` on Unknown Member, code 10007 (https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/opcodes-and-status-codes, read 2026-10-23).
- **Retries.** Through `DiscordApi` and `DiscordOAuth`, a 429 is retried once, after its `retry_after`, when that is 2 s or less. Nothing else is retried, since a create that got a 5xx may have landed. Every call they make gives up after 10 s, through `AbortSignal.timeout`. An alert follows its own rules (**Monitoring**). The panel tracks no rate-limit bucket: it only reacts to a 429.
- **Write results.** A domain write checks every limit Discord documents before calling it, so a 400 from Discord is a panel error. It re-reads what it changes right before writing and applies only its own change, with no conflict error: a form sends one word, never a list. It returns `{ ok: true, message }`, or `{ ok: false, message }` with each field the form sent, as typed and under its name, such as `word`: one French `message` per form, written by the domain, that says what landed, such as « « foo » est maintenant un mot interdit. », or what went wrong, for refused input and for Discord unavailable, and the fields so the form keeps them (**Interface**). The action hands that to the form as is. A timeout that lands returns `{ ok: true }` whatever its direct message then meets, since the sanction holds: its message says that the member did not receive the direct message when Discord refuses it, with 50007, 50278 or 40003, or may not have when Discord is unavailable, and a direct message that fails otherwise is thrown, as a bug of the panel, so that it alerts, though the timeout has landed (Gilles, 2026-10-28, on #179, whose « S'il échoue, […] le résultat dit que le membre ne l'a pas reçu » this narrows to Discord's refusals and to Discord unavailable). A kick sends its direct message first, since a kicked account no longer shares a Discord server with the application (#180): the kick then happens whatever the message met, and the result says what the message met, as for a timeout; Discord unavailable during the kick returns `{ ok: false }`, saying that the kick may or may not have landed, and that the message went, if it did, with a reminder to untick the direct message before retrying, since a retry would send it again, while Discord unavailable before the kick says it did not happen; and a panel error is thrown once the kick was tried, during the kick though the message went, or during the message though the kick landed (Gilles, 2026-10-28, on #180, whose « Si l'expulsion échoue ensuite, le résultat dit que le message est parti mais pas la sanction » this narrows to Discord unavailable, as for #179; that a message's panel error waits for the kick is inferred from #180's « Si le message échoue, l'expulsion a lieu quand même »). Anything else is thrown, a `DiscordError` from the action's `requireStaffMember` included. Reads never write: what a page needs created, the first write creates.
- **Read failures.** What a page reads from Discord is thrown on failure, with no catch on the way, and `error.tsx` replaces the page under its layout. In production it gets a generic message and the error's `digest`, never the original message (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/03-file-conventions/error.md:106`, `:111-115`), so it shows one message for Discord unavailable and panel error alike, a « Réessayer » button that calls `retry`, and the `digest` as a reference. One read is exempt: a lookup of a member's avatar in the audit log that throws a `DiscordError` shows the member's initials instead, as for a member no longer on the Discord server, since an avatar is decorative, while any other error it throws is thrown (**Avatars**, #129, #141). So a panel error there alerts no one, against **Alerts**: the page's staff check reads its own member first, through the same `getMember` and the same token (`src/app/(staff)/audit-log/page.tsx:39`, `:43`), so a revoked token or a lost permission fails there and alerts, and only a failure tied to one member's ID, such as an ID that names no account, goes unreported (inferred; Gilles, 2026-10-28, on #141).
- **Error pages.** `src/app/` holds `not-found.tsx`, `error.tsx` and `global-error.tsx` with their own markup, since they belong to no domain. Next.js requires `error.tsx` and `global-error.tsx` to be Client Components, and `global-error.tsx` to bring its own `<html>` and `<body>` (`error.md:21`, `:163`), here with `lang="fr"`. The login callback throws nothing: a member who is not staff goes to `/login/refused`, any other failure to `/login/failed`.
- **Messages** are the panel's own French sentences, never Discord's message, a stack or a token. A failure's message says what to do next: correct the input, retry, or report the problem in the staff's Discord channel, named in the text (`docs/agents/open-questions.md`). A success's message says what landed (**Write results**).

## Testing

What the panel's tests prove and where, as Gilles decided while grilling #54 on 2026-10-23, then #53 for the theme and accessibility, and #50 for monitoring. Like **Architecture**, it was written before the code.

- **Layers.** Domain functions carry most of the tests, in Vitest against the fake Discord. The real implementations of `src/discord/` are tested on their own (**The real implementations**). Entry points only wire (**Architecture**): Playwright covers the flows where a wiring mistake shows nowhere else (**End to end**), and nothing but the typecheck and the build checks the rest. No coverage tool, no threshold, no snapshot: a test names the text or the attribute it proves, never a class, but for `sr-only`, the one class that says a text is for assistive technologies alone, as the header's test does for the menu button's « Ouvrir le menu » (Gilles, 2026-10-27, on #137).
- **The fake.** `fake-discord.ts` implements `DiscordApi` and `DiscordOAuth`. Its constructor takes a Discord server's starting state: members, with their roles, their avatar on the server, their arrival and the end of their timeout; accounts that are not on it; bans, with their reason; roles, beside the `@everyone` it always holds; AutoMod rules; audit log entries (#176); the Discord server's owner, the roles of the application, which is always a member, and the members who refuse direct messages (#179). Its search of members matches the start of a username or a nickname, whatever its case, as Discord did (`pnpm test:contract`, 2026-10-28). What happens in Discord outside the panel has methods of its own, named as in Discord, such as a member losing a role, or a member creating or editing an AutoMod rule by hand: the audit log records that rule change under the member's name, with no reason from the panel. So does a member who times out a member, lifts their timeout or changes their nickname, kicks one, or bans an account or lifts its ban, by hand, as `MEMBER_UPDATE`, `MEMBER_KICK`, `MEMBER_BAN_ADD` or `MEMBER_BAN_REMOVE`, the events Discord's docs name for them, whose `user_id` is the "User or app that made the changes" (https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/audit-log, read 2026-10-28), with the reason the member typed, if any (inferred for a change by hand); a kick or a ban's lifting leaves an account that is not on the Discord server (#177). AutoMod times out a member through a rule whose action is a timeout, for that action's duration, as `AUTO_MODERATION_USER_COMMUNICATION_DISABLED`, whose optional info holds the rule's name and trigger type (the same page). That page says neither what such an entry's `user_id` holds nor whether AutoMod's timeout also adds a `MEMBER_UPDATE`, and that its `target_id` is the member is inferred from its description, "Member was timed out by Auto Moderation": the fake sets that `user_id` to the member, which the panel does not read, and adds no `MEMBER_UPDATE`, which would have the history show the timeout twice, and the audit log show it among the sanctions the staff take, which #182 leaves out (inferred, #177, #209). Its audit log's `users` hold every account an entry's `user_id` or `target_id` names, a former member included, as Discord's held the member a timeout targets (`pnpm test:contract`, 2026-10-29; inferred for a kick, a ban and a former member, #182). Another method issues the OAuth2 code a test logs in with, for the code challenge of that login, and the exchange refuses the code with another code verifier, or none, as Discord did (**OAuth2**). The fake refuses what Discord refuses among the limits the MVP touches: 6 keyword rules per Discord server and, per rule, 1,000 keywords of up to 60 characters (ADR 0002). It throws the `DiscordError` of a 400 with code 50035, so a limit the domain forgets to check fails a test. A timeout it refuses as Discord does (#179): the owner, as Discord did, and a member with the Administrator permission, against whom Modify Guild Member "Will throw a 403 error" (https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/guild, modified 2026-10-27), or whose highest role equals or exceeds the application's, since a bot acts only on "users whose highest role is lower than the bot's highest role" (https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/permissions, modified 2026-08-13), each with a 403 of code 50013, whose code those two cases are inferred to share with the owner's, and an end more than 28 days ahead with that 400, as Discord did (`pnpm test:contract`, 2026-10-28). It records a timeout or its lifting in its audit log as an entry `MEMBER_UPDATE`, whose change `communication_disabled_until` holds the old end and the new one, the lifting no new one, as Discord did (the same run). A direct message it refuses with a 403 of code 50007, "Cannot send messages to this user", to a member who refuses them, and of code 50278, "Cannot send messages to this user due to having no mutual guilds", to an account that is not a member (https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/opcodes-and-status-codes, modified 2026-10-27; the status 403 is inferred), and one over 2,000 characters, the limit Discord documents for a webhook's `content` (**What an alert says**), with that 400 (inferred for a direct message); `directMessagesTo(userId)` reads what an account received, which no method of `DiscordApi` reads. It kicks a member as `MEMBER_KICK`, under the application and with the panel's reason, and keeps their account as one that is not on the Discord server, as Discord did (trial under `pnpm dev`, 2026-10-29, #215); it refuses, with that 403 of code 50013, to kick the owner or a member whose highest role equals or exceeds the application's, though not one with Administrator, whom Discord's rule for a bot does not name, and answers an account that is not a member with Unknown Member (inferred from the same pages, since the kick stays out of the contract suite, #180). An audit log reason, which Discord documents as 1 to 512 characters (ADR 0004), it refuses neither way: it keeps a longer one's first 512 characters, and an empty one as no reason at all (`pnpm test:contract`, 2026-10-25). The fake does the same, so a test that reads its reason back from the audit log sees the cut (Gilles, 2026-10-25, on #59). `failNext(method, error)` makes that method's next call throw that `DiscordError`, once. A test that needs a method to fail with anything else, as a bug in the panel would, subclasses the fake in its own file and overrides that method, as the audit log's test of a member lookup does (#141). ## Security

How the panel protects the staff's session, its secrets and its writes, and how it reads its configuration, as Gilles decided while grilling #51 on 2026-10-24. Like **Architecture**, it was written before the code: #59 sets up the configuration, Discord's variables and `server-only`, #10 the staff's variables, the session and the login, #63 `E2E_TEST_PROXY`, #76 the headers, and #9 what lives in Vercel's dashboard. The options set aside, and why, are in #51's decisions.

- **The session** is a cookie that iron-session 9 seals, encrypted and signed, holding only the member's Discord ID (ADR 0005); no session lives on the server, since the panel stores nothing (ADR 0004). Auth.js and Better Auth are set aside: each brings its own OAuth flow, which would replace `DiscordOAuth` and the two Route Handlers. It is named `__Host-session`, with `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax` and `Path=/`, in development too, since Chromium takes a `Secure` cookie on `http://localhost` (end to end, 2026-10-26). `Lax`, not `Strict`: the return from Discord to `/login/callback`, and a link to the panel clicked in Discord, are navigations that start on another site, which a `Strict` cookie skips. `vercel.app` is on the Public Suffix List (https://publicsuffix.org/list/public_suffix_list.dat, line 16331, read 2026-10-24), so another Vercel project is another site, which can neither set the panel's cookies nor send them on a POST; `__Host-` costs nothing, and would matter under a domain of the panel's own. iron-session 9.0.1 needs Node `>=22.13.0` and is ESM only (`npm view iron-session`, 2026-10-24).
- **Its lifetime** is 7 days from the login, written into the seal's `ttl` and the cookie's `Max-Age`, and never extended: a Server Component cannot set a cookie (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/04-functions/cookies.md:81`), so extending it would mean writing the cookie from `proxy.ts` or from every Server Action. A session ends when it expires, through « Se déconnecter », or, for every member at once, when `SESSION_SECRET` changes. None can be revoked on its own (iron-session 9.0.1's README, « How to invalidate sessions? », `npm view iron-session readme`, 2026-10-24): the staff check on every request is what cuts a member's access (ADR 0005), so a stolen cookie is worth at most 7 days, and only while its member holds the staff role.
- **OAuth2.** `/login` draws 32 random bytes as the `state`, in base64url, keeps them in a `__Host-oauth-state` cookie with `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax` and a `Max-Age` of 10 minutes, and sends `prompt=consent`: Discord documents no default for `prompt`, and `none` would send a member who clicks « Se reconnecter » back as the same account (https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/oauth2, modified 2026-10-16). The callback compares the `state` in constant time, deletes its cookie whatever the outcome, and goes to `/login/failed` when it is missing or differs, so of two logins started in two tabs, only the later one can succeed, and only if it returns first. A login always lands on `/`, with no return path in the URL, so no redirect is open. PKCE with S256, which Discord's metadata lists (`code_challenge_methods_supported`, https://discord.com/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server, read 2026-10-24) but its OAuth2 page does not document, stays: on 2026-10-26, against « Shor Panel Dev », Discord refused a code exchanged with a wrong `code_verifier` (`400 invalid_grant`, « Code challenge failed. ») or with none (`400 invalid_request`, « Missing "code_verifier" »), and accepted it with the right one (#10). So `/login` also draws 32 random bytes as the `code_verifier`, in base64url, sends their S256 challenge, and keeps them in the same cookie, after the `state` and a dot, for the callback's exchange. `state` stays all the same, as #51 decided, since RFC 9700 relies on PKCE against CSRF only once the server is known to support it (§4.7.1). The member's token is not revoked after `/users/@me`: it never leaves the callback's memory, and revoking it would revoke every token of that authorization (the OAuth2 page), for one more call and one more failure. `iss`, which Discord has returned since 2026-10-14 (https://docs.discord.com/developers/change-log), goes unchecked: Discord advises the check for a client of several providers.
- **CSRF.** Nothing beyond what Next.js and the `Lax` cookie give: no token, and no `serverActions.allowedOrigins`. A Server Action takes only POST, and its `Origin` must match `Host` (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/data-security.md:548-550`). A POST without `Origin` passes with a warning (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/05-config/01-next-config-js/serverActions.md:13`), but a browser sends `Origin` on a POST, and a client that does not carries no session cookie (inferred). `/login` and `/login/callback` are GETs that write nothing to Discord, and `state` covers the login itself.
- **Headers.** `next.config.ts` sets, on every route, a Content Security Policy without nonces (ADR 0006), `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; img-src 'self' data: blob: https://cdn.discordapp.com; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; upgrade-insecure-requests`, with `'unsafe-eval'` added to `script-src` in development only, when `NODE_ENV` is `development` (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/content-security-policy.md:548`); then `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, all through `headers()` on `/:path*`, and `poweredByHeader: false`, so no response carries `X-Powered-By`. `img-src` allows `https://cdn.discordapp.com` for the members' avatars (**Avatars**, #140). `next.config.test.ts` proves both policies, and an end-to-end test reads the headers on `/login/logged-out` (#76). `upgrade-insecure-requests` holds in every environment: under `next start` on `http://localhost:3100`, Chromium still loaded every script and stylesheet over `http:`, and every end-to-end flow passed (throwaway test, 2026-10-27); only the target of a redirect that a `fetch` follows went to `https:` (**Gotchas**). Under `next dev`, the same flows logged no violation (throwaway Playwright config, 2026-10-27), though two failed on Next.js's own development warning « Route … is rendering with server caches disabled », which `next start` does not print, and the headers test, as it should, on the development policy. HSTS is Vercel's: `max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains; preload` on `*.vercel.app` (https://vercel.com/docs/cdn-security/encryption, updated 2026-09-15). Since scripts may run inline, React's escaping is the defense against XSS, and `react/no-danger` and `react/jsx-no-script-url` are errors, the latter on `<a>` and on `next/link`'s `Link`, which it skips without being named (#76, 2026-10-27). The Vercel Toolbar, on by default on previews, is turned off there (#9), so previews serve production's policy rather than one opened to `vercel.live` (https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-toolbar/managing-toolbar, updated 2026-08-11).
- **Rate limiting.** One rule of the Vercel Firewall, the only one Hobby allows per project (https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-firewall/vercel-waf/rate-limiting, updated 2026-08-28), set in the dashboard by #9: every path, keyed on the IP, 600 requests over a fixed window of 10 minutes, then deny. A Hobby project past an included limit waits 30 days (https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby, updated 2026-09-14), and a function invocation counts "regardless of request success or failure" (https://vercel.com/docs/functions/usage-and-pricing, updated 2026-06-16), so a script from one IP could take the panel down; traffic the Firewall denies counts toward no usage (https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-firewall/vercel-waf/usage-and-pricing, updated 2026-06-16). Attack Mode, free on every plan, answers a flood from many IPs (https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-firewall/attack-mode, updated 2026-08-11). The panel's code limits nothing: a write needs a session whose member holds the staff role, and Discord's 429 is handled (**Retries**).
- **The application's permissions.** The panel's application never holds Administrator. Each feature adds the Discord permissions and privileged intents it needs, and no more, in the same change as the README's list of them, and the panel's role sits just above the roles it manages. A staff member who holds Manage Server on Shor's Discord server grants them, since Gilles does not (#8). "Shor Panel Dev" gets a wave's permissions on Gilles' test Discord server ahead of its features, through a ticket of its own, for `pnpm dev` and the contract suite, as #178 gave it the moderation's (#143) and #192 the interactions wave's (#191). Whoever holds the application's token holds all of them, so each one widens what a leak costs (Gilles, 2026-10-27, while grilling the features that follow the MVP).
- **Secrets.** Only `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variables reach the browser (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/environment-variables.md:156-164`), and the panel has none. Every module that reads a secret from `process.env` or calls Discord starts with `import "server-only"`, so a client component that reaches it fails the build. Next.js resolves `server-only` itself (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/01-getting-started/05-server-and-client-components.md:602`), but Vitest does not, and the package's default entry throws, only its `react-server` condition leading to an empty module (`npm view server-only exports`, 2026-10-24): `server-only` is a dependency, and `vitest.config.mts` aliases it to that empty module. React's taint APIs stay off, being experimental (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/data-security.md:219-236`).
- **Personal data.** [`docs/personal-data.md`](docs/personal-data.md) says what the panel processes about people, why, where and for how long, and what the GDPR and Discord's Developer Terms ask before the store opens. A change that starts or stops processing a personal datum updates it in the same change (Gilles, 2026-10-28, while grilling #27).
- **CI secrets.** `ci.yml` holds none. "Shor Panel Dev"'s token is CI's one secret, in the GitHub environment `discord-contract`, which only `contract.yml` uses (**Delivery**, #102). On Gilles' test Discord server, its application held only Manage Server and View Audit Log (#10, comment of 2026-10-23), and since #178 also holds Timeout Members, Kick Members and Ban Members, and since #192 Manage Roles and Manage Channels, through a role right under the staff role and above the test Discord server's Membre role, beyond what `@everyone` gives every member (`GET /guilds/{guild.id}/roles` and `GET /guilds/{guild.id}/members/{user.id}`, 2026-10-28). GitHub Free offers environments to public repositories only (https://docs.github.com/en/actions/managing-workflow-runs-and-deployments/managing-deployments/managing-environments-for-deployment, read 2026-10-25), and this one has been public since 2026-10-27 (ADR 0007, #100).
- **Configuration.** Each folder validates its own variables with zod, in a module without `server-only` that exports a function parsing the environment it is given; `src/discord/env.ts` has one per client, since `contract.yml` holds only `DiscordApi`'s variables (Gilles, 2026-10-25, on #59), and a third, `parseTestProxyEnv`, for `E2E_TEST_PROXY`, the switch that lets the HTTP adapter answer for Discord end to end (Gilles, 2026-10-25, on #63). The folder's factories call it on `process.env` when they first run, in development as in production, and a failed parse is then a panel error (**Four kinds of failure**). When `VERCEL` is set, `next.config.ts` also calls every folder's at build, so a missing or invalid variable fails the build and Vercel keeps the previous production deployment: "Your production domains don't serve a deployment until a production deployment succeeds" (https://vercel.com/docs/deployments/environments, updated 2026-09-17). Next.js loads `.env*` before `next.config.ts` (`node_modules/next/dist/server/config.js:1263`), and Vercel gives a project's variables to its build (https://vercel.com/docs/environment-variables, updated 2026-09-17). Elsewhere the build checks nothing, since `pnpm typecheck` loads `next.config.ts` too (**Gotchas**): a fresh clone or a worktree, without `.env.local`, still typechecks and builds, and `ci.yml` needs no placeholder values. `E2E_TEST_PROXY`, which `next.config.ts` parses on every load, may be absent. A failed parse's message names the variable, never its value: zod 4 leaves the input out of its issues (https://zod.dev/error-customization, read 2026-10-24), and the panel turns on no `reportInput` and adds no error map that would put it back.
- **Variables.** `src/discord/` reads `DISCORD_CLIENT_ID`, `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET` and `DISCORD_REDIRECT_URI` for `DiscordOAuth`, and `DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN` and `DISCORD_GUILD_ID` for `DiscordApi`; `src/discord/`'s contract suite also reads `CONTRACT_MEMBER_ID`, through `parseContractEnv`, the member of the test Discord server it times out and sends a direct message (#179); `src/staff/` reads `STAFF_ROLE_ID`, `MODERATION_ROLE_IDS`, `BAN_ROLE_IDS` and `SESSION_SECRET`; `src/monitoring/` reads `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL`, and Vercel's `VERCEL_ENV` and `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA` (**Monitoring**); `next.config.ts` reads `E2E_TEST_PROXY`, and the `NODE_ENV` that Next.js sets, for the Content Security Policy (**Headers**). IDs are snowflakes of 17 to 20 digits (inferred from Discord's 64-bit IDs). `MODERATION_ROLE_IDS` and `BAN_ROLE_IDS` each list one or more of them, split on commas, with spaces allowed around each (ADR 0005, #176). `DISCORD_REDIRECT_URI` is a URL, `https:` except on `localhost`, set per environment, and on a preview only on demand (**Delivery**, **Previews**). `SESSION_SECRET` holds at least 32 characters (the same README, `password`). `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` is a URL under `https://discord.com/api/webhooks/`, required when `VERCEL_ENV` is `production` and left unset elsewhere, where `Monitor` only writes to the console; an empty one counts as unset, so that a copy of `.env.example` sends no alert rather than failing its parse (Gilles, 2026-10-25, on #78, where `E2E_TEST_PROXY` refuses an empty value and stays out of `.env.example`). `VERCEL_ENV` and `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA` are system variables, which Vercel sets once the project exposes them (#9), so they are absent outside Vercel and stay out of `.env.example`. The others are non-empty. `DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN` and `DISCORD_GUILD_ID` take Discord's names, as `src/discord/` does (`docs/agents/domain.md`): Discord files that token under its application's Bot tab and sends it as `Authorization: Bot`, though the docs call it the panel's application token (ADR 0003). `STAFF_ROLE_ID` names one role until `docs/agents/open-questions.md` says whether others make a member staff. `E2E_TEST_PROXY` is absent or `1`, and the build refuses it when `VERCEL` is set, as Vercel does at build and at runtime (https://vercel.com/docs/environment-variables/system-environment-variables, updated 2026-07-15) once the project exposes its system variables, which #9 checks. `.env.example` lists them all, empty, but Vercel's two and `E2E_TEST_PROXY`, which only Playwright's `webServer` sets and which an empty value copied from it would break (Gilles, 2026-10-25, on #63), and the README documents each one as the code that reads it arrives.

## Monitoring

What the panel logs, what it never logs, and how its maintainer learns that it broke, as Gilles decided while grilling #50 on 2026-10-24, with everything kept free. Like **Architecture**, it was written before the code: #78 wrote `src/monitoring/` and `instrumentation.ts`, #59 gave `DiscordError` its route's pattern, #10 makes the login callback report what it catches, and #9 sets the webhook's variable in Vercel's dashboard. The options set aside, and why, are in #50's decisions.

- **Logs and the audit log.** Logs say why the panel failed, for whoever maintains it; the audit log says which staff member changed what (`GLOSSARY.md`, ADR 0004). So logs hold failures only: no staff action and no success, neither a write that lands, which Discord's audit log records with the panel's reason, nor a login.
- **What is logged.** Next.js prints every error thrown in a render, a Server Action or a Route Handler itself, through `console.error`, before it calls `onRequestError` (`node_modules/next/dist/server/route-modules/route-module.js:320-327`), with its message, its stack and its enumerable properties, `digest` included (`node_modules/next/dist/server/patch-error-inspect.js:437-442`); `redirect()` and `notFound()` never get there (`node_modules/next/dist/server/app-render/create-error-handler.js:52`, `:68-71`). The panel adds no line to those. An error of `proxy.ts` takes another way to `onRequestError`, with no such `console.error` on it (`node_modules/next/dist/build/templates/middleware.js:108-118`), and that way looks for the instrumentation the edge runtime loads, `_ENTRIES.middleware_instrumentation` (`node_modules/next/dist/server/web/globals.js:27-30`, `:48-57`), while `proxy.ts` runs on Node.js only (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/03-file-conventions/proxy.md:255`). On 2026-10-25, a throwaway `proxy.ts` that threw, under `next start`, never reached `onRequestError`, and Next.js printed its error once, with its stack (inferred for Vercel, where it was not tried). So `proxy.ts` catches what it throws and hands it to `Monitor.error`, which writes the one line and sends the alert, then answers without throwing it again (Gilles, 2026-10-25, on #78), by letting the request through: `proxy.ts` authorizes nothing, and each page runs its own check, which fails the same way and alerts a second time (Gilles, 2026-10-26, on #10). Beyond that, the panel writes only what the login callback catches, since the callback throws nothing (**Error pages**): a panel error at `error`, Discord unavailable and a missing or differing `state` at `warn`. Refused input and refused access leave nothing, nor does a login the member cancels on Discord (Gilles, 2026-10-26, on #10), nor a write that returns `{ ok: false }` because Discord is unavailable, nor a 429 retried with success. `info` and `debug` go unused.
- **What is never logged**, nor sent in an alert: a secret, such as `DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN`, `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET`, `SESSION_SECRET` or `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL`; a member's OAuth2 token; the callback's `code` and `state`; a cookie, or the `Authorization` and `Cookie` headers; a member's Discord ID, name or IP, since the panel stores no personal data (ADR 0004); and what the staff typed, such as a blocked word. A member who reports a failure names themself in the staff's channel. That is why a `DiscordError` carries its route's pattern, never the path filled in (**`DiscordError`**).
- **Log lines** go through `console.error` and `console.warn`, called with the error object as Next.js does, with no logging library and no JSON. Vercel's own pages disagree on whether the fields of a JSON line are searchable (https://vercel.com/docs/logs/runtime, updated 2026-08-28, against https://vercel.com/kb/guide/add-structured-application-logs-to-vercel-functions, updated 2026-05-28), and Hobby keeps runtime logs one hour (the first page), so the alert carries what matters.
- **Alerts.** Every panel error notifies Gilles, the maintainer, alone, but one that a lookup of an avatar in the audit log turns into initials (**Read failures**), and stays findable for at least 7 days, one thrown in `proxy.ts` included, which `proxy.ts` reports itself (**What is logged**); Discord unavailable notifies no one, since it passes. The staff get no alert: they report what they see in their channel (**Messages**), `error.tsx` with its reference, or `/login/failed`, which shows none. Alerts go through a Discord webhook into a private channel of Gilles' test Discord server (#7): it is free, needs no other account, and the panel writes every word it sends. Discord keeps a channel's messages with no expiry, which covers the 7 days (inferred, not checked against Discord's docs). Executing a webhook is a call to Discord's REST API (ADR 0003), and the one call to Discord outside `DiscordApi` and `DiscordOAuth` (**The Discord seam**). Nothing is grouped: ten failing pages send ten messages, and an alert sent during a Discord outage is lost.
- **What an alert says**, in English like the logs (**Language**): the environment, from `VERCEL_ENV`, `local` outside Vercel, and the short commit, from `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA`; the context's `routeType` and `routePath`, the route's file path (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/03-file-conventions/instrumentation.md:109`), when Next.js gives one; the `digest`, when there is one; the error's name and message, which for a `DiscordError` hold its method, its route's pattern, its status, and Discord's `code` and `message` (`src/discord/discord-error.ts`); then the stack's frames. It is one `content`, with no embed, of at most Discord's 2,000 characters (https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/webhook, modified 2026-06-25): those lines, then the error and its frames in a code block, whose end is cut, ending on `…`, when it does not fit. Never `request.path`, which holds the query string (`instrumentation.md:103`), and so the callback's `code` and `state`, nor anything else **What is never logged** names. It goes with `allowed_mentions: { parse: [] }`, so that no error text mentions anyone, where a webhook's default parses users (the message page). - **Sending.** An alert never changes what the staff see. Next.js awaits `onRequestError` before it answers a Route Handler or an error that escapes a render (`node_modules/next/dist/build/templates/app-route.js:338`, `node_modules/next/dist/build/templates/app-page-runtime.js:1602`), but not for an error thrown while streaming (`create-error-handler.js:125`), and `after()` is documented for Server Components, Server Functions, Route Handlers and `proxy.ts` only (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/04-functions/after.md:8`). So the alert runs in the background, gives up after 2 s through `AbortSignal.timeout`, and never throws: a failed send is one `console.error` line. It is never retried, a 429 included: in a burst the same error repeats, so the first alerts carry it (inferred). Discord gives no figure for the rate limit of webhooks (https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/rate-limits, modified 2026-02-06). - **`src/monitoring/`** sits outside the domain, like `src/discord/` and `src/ui/`. It exposes `Monitor`, one method per level, which `createMonitor()` builds, in `monitor.ts`, without reading its variables: `error` writes the line, then reads them and sends the alert, `warn` writes the line only. So a request that reports nothing, through `proxy.ts` say, never fails on `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` (#10). `isPanelError`, in `panel-error.ts`, tells whether a thrown error alerts: a `DiscordError` that says Discord is unavailable does not, anything else does. It knows a `DiscordError` by its `name` and its `unavailable`, not by `instanceof`, since `instrumentation.ts` gets a copy of the class of its own (**Gotchas**). `src/instrumentation.ts` only wires: its `onRequestError` hands the error, the request and the context to the reporter that `createRequestErrorReporter()` builds, in `request-error.ts`, which sends the alert when `isPanelError` says so, and writes no line, since Next.js has written it. The login callback's domain function takes a `Monitor`, as it takes `DiscordApi` and `DiscordOAuth`, and so does what `proxy.ts` calls. `alert-message.ts` lays out the alert, and `alert.ts` sends it, over a `fetch` and a `waitUntil` taken as parameters, like **The real implementations**, which the two factories fill with `globalThis.fetch` and `@vercel/functions`' `waitUntil`, behind `import "server-only"` (**Secrets**). `env.ts` reads `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL`, `VERCEL_ENV` and `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA` (**Variables**). `InMemoryMonitor`, in `in-memory-monitor.ts`, keeps each report with its level for the tests, and the `no-restricted-imports` rule that keeps the fake Discord in `*.test.*` files (**The Discord seam**) refuses it elsewhere too.
- **Stacks** stay as Next.js builds them, minified, since `experimental.serverMinification` defaults to `true` (`node_modules/next/dist/server/config-shared.js:203`): the panel adds neither `serverSourceMaps` nor `--enable-source-maps`. The message, the route and a `DiscordError`'s fields place most failures in a panel this small. This is revisited when a stack proves unreadable.
- **No uptime probe.** The failures that matter, such as a revoked token or a removed permission, show only when a page reads Discord, so when the staff use the panel, and the alert fires then. A failed deployment notifies its creator, on the web and by email, by default on every plan (https://vercel.com/docs/notifications, updated 2026-09-14), and Vercel keeps the previous production deployment (**Configuration**).
- **The browser reports nothing.** Client leaves hold no logic (**Components**), an action's error is thrown on the server before the browser sees it, and `connect-src` stays `'self'` (**Headers**).

- **Deprecated packages.** pnpm prints a deprecation only at `pnpm add` and `pnpm update` (**Warnings**), and records it as a `deprecated:` field of `pnpm-lock.yaml`, which held none on 2026-10-25. - **The registry.** Each session runs `pnpm exec shadcn add <component> --diff` on every copied component. What the registry changed comes in like a minor update, and the panel's own lines stay (**Copied code** of **Interface** and of **Code style**).
- **Adding a dependency.** Before `pnpm add`, its `engines`, which must hold `engines.node` (**Gotchas**), its `peerDependencies`, and whether it is deprecated, are read, and one that reaches a hosted service when it installs or runs first gets its entry in `docs/costs.md` (**Costs**). - **The session's PR.** One per session for all but the majors, on `dependencies/<yyyy-mm>`. ## Commits, PRs, issues

- Commit messages read `Area: what changed`, in English.
- A branch is named `<area>/<subject>`: the area of the commits it carries, in lower case, such as `docs`, `tooling`, `interface`, `repo` or `dependencies` (**Dependencies**), then a few English words in kebab-case, with no issue number, since the PR gives it. A session renames its branch before its first push, since the desktop app names the branches of new sessions after a prefix of its own. Review checks it (Gilles, 2026-10-25, while grilling #57).
- The user is the sole author: commits carry no `Co-Authored-By` trailer, PRs carry no "Generated with" footer.
- Only the maintainer, Gilles (`docs/agents/issue-tracker.md`, **People**), merges into `main`, with a merge commit, never a squash. The repository allows merge commits only, since #100 turned squash and rebase off (**The rules of `main`**). Vercel's Hobby plan has no team collaboration, and deploys no private repository of a GitHub organization (https://vercel.com/docs/git, updated 2026-09-18), hence a public repository (ADR 0007). Its check that a commit's author owns the Hobby team is written for private repositories (same page, **Deploying private Git repositories**), and Gilles authors every commit anyway.
- Issues, PR descriptions and comments read like a teammate wrote them: direct, specific, complete, no filler.
- A PR that only advances a ticket names it once, as `Part of #<n>`. GitHub closes, at merge, every issue that a PR description or commit message puts right after `close`, `fix` or `resolve` (any form), even inside a negation.
- Once a PR merges, delete its local branch with `git branch -d` and run `git fetch --prune`. When `-d` refuses, the branch holds unmerged work: ask the user.

## Gotchas

- `@babel/parser`, which the comment check uses, stays on 7.x: 8.x requires Node `^22.18.0 || >=24.11.0` (`npm view @babel/parser@8 engines`, 2026-10-23), narrower than the `^22.13.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0` that `engines` promises in `package.json`. CI would not catch the gap: its `node-version: 22` ran Node 22.23.3 on 2026-10-23 (run 39944674281), which 8.x accepts.
- `engines.node` is the intersection of the ranges the toolchain declares: Vitest 5 wants `^22.12.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`, ESLint 10 `^20.19.0 || ^22.13.0 || >=24` (`npm view <package> engines`, 2026-10-23). Read a new dependency's `engines` before adding it: jsdom 30, for one, wants `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`.
- `typescript@7` on npm is the native compiler: its package exports no stable JavaScript API (only `./unstable/*`), so tooling that parses code cannot rely on it. That is why the comment check parses with Babel, and why TypeScript stays on `~6.0.3`: typescript-eslint, which `eslint-config-next/typescript` uses, accepts `typescript >=4.8.4 <6.1.0` (8.72.0, `npm view typescript-eslint peerDependencies`, 2026-10-23). Next.js alone would accept 7, through its `tsc` CLI (https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/config/typescript, updated 2026-08-25).
- ESLint is on 10, since 9.x reached end-of-life on 2026-08-06 (https://eslint.org/version-support, read 2026-10-23). `eslint-config-next` supports 10 since 16.4.0 (https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/config/eslint, updated 2026-10-15), but three plugins it bundles, `eslint-plugin-import`, `eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y` and `eslint-plugin-react`, still cap their `eslint` peer at `^9`: hence the `peerDependencyRules` of `pnpm-workspace.yaml`. Drop each rule once its plugin lists 10.
- pnpm 12's `minimumReleaseAge` defaults to 1,440 minutes, and stays non-strict until the setting is written down (https://github.com/pnpm/pnpm/blob/v12.9.1/pnpm/crates/config/src/registry_options.rs#L200-L212).
