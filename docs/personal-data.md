# Personal data

What the Admin Panel processes about people, why, where and for how long, and what the GDPR and Discord's terms ask of it before it stores more, as Gilles decided while grilling #27 on 2026-10-28. A change that starts or stops processing a personal datum updates this file, and the privacy policy at `/privacy`, which says what **Today** says in French, with the date it shows, in the same change (`CLAUDE.md`, **Security**, **Privacy policy**). Every page below was read on 2026-10-28; a date after a URL is the page's own. Nobody on the project is a lawyer: the inferences are a developer's reading of the sources, worth checking with the CNIL or a lawyer before the store opens.

## What applies

- **The GDPR.** Personal data is « toute information se rapportant à une personne physique identifiée ou identifiable » (art. 4(1)), and the Regulation leaves out a processing « par une personne physique dans le cadre d'une activité strictement personnelle ou domestique » (art. 2(2)(c)) (https://www.cnil.fr/fr/reglement-europeen-protection-donnees/chapitre1). A Discord ID names an account, so what the panel reads or keeps about one is personal data, and a staff that runs a community is no natural person acting privately, so the exemption likely does not cover it (inferred).
- **The CNIL's guide for associations** (`cnil-guide_association.pdf`, dated 2021). Associations are subject to the GDPR, and legitimate interest is among their legal bases. What they tell people states the retention period and « au moins les droits d'accès, de rectification, d'effacement ». An association keeps a record of its processing, and of its former members: « Elle doit les supprimer trois ans après la fin de l'adhésion de la personne. » The CNIL publishes no guidance for moderation or sanction records.
- **Discord's Developer Terms of Service** (Wayback Machine copy of 2026-09-27, « Last updated: June 6, 2024 ») cover "API Data", "any data… you obtain through the APIs (including personal data)". Every application keeps a privacy policy, links it from the Developer Portal, and makes it "easily accessible to users from your Application" (§5a). "Except to the extent you are required to retain API Data under applicable laws or regulations", it deletes that data once no longer necessary, when it stops, when Discord asks, or when "the applicable user requests you delete it", and gives "users an easily accessible way to ask for their API Data to be modified and deleted" (§5b). It encrypts the data at rest (§5c).
- **Discord's Developer Policy** (Wayback Machine copy of 2026-10-27): "Do not contact users on Discord without their explicit permission. This includes frequently sending unsolicited direct messages and/or sending direct messages not directly related to maintaining or improving an Application's functionality" (§5), and "Do not use API Data for any purpose outside of what is necessary to provide your stated functionality" (§15). A direct message about a sanction the staff give is related to that functionality (inferred).

## Today

What the panel processes on `main`, and with the moderation section that stores nothing (#143):

- **A staff member's Discord ID**, sealed in the `__Host-session` cookie, in their browser only, for 7 days at most (ADR 0005, `CLAUDE.md`, **The session**), to keep them logged in.
- **What the panel reads from Discord and shows without keeping it**: the staff member's name and avatar in the header, the staff behind each change in the audit log (ADR 0004), and there, to every staff member, the account each timeout, kick or ban targets, or whose timeout or ban is lifted, with its reason, and the account each automatic timeout targets, which AutoMod gives when a message trips one of its rules, with the name of that rule (#327), while only those who moderate get the link to its record (Gilles, 2026-10-29, on #182), as they alone get the link to the record of the staff member behind each change, and of the staff member shown in the navigation (#320), and, with the moderation section, an account's names, avatar, roles, dates, standing and the audit log entries about it (#176, #177). A record's URL holds the account's ID, `/records/{userId}`; the list of accounts', the text searched, a role's ID, the filters, the sort and the page, `/records?query=…&role=…&standing=…&joined=…&created=…&bots=…&sort=…&page=2`, where the text is a name, an ID or a mention, which the browser's history keeps too (#322, #324); and the audit log's, the author and the account searched, `/audit-log?author=…&account=…`, a name, an ID or a mention, which the browser's history keeps too (#176, #321); and the support tickets', the filters, `/support-tickets?kind=…&opener=…&closer=…&reason=…&from=…&to=…&on=…&sort=…&page=2`, where the opener and the closer are a name, an ID or a mention and the reason holds words, which the browser's history keeps too (#386).
- **The list of accounts of `/records`** (#322). At each view, the panel reads every member of the Discord server, every banned account and the Discord server's roles, and shows to the staff who moderate each one's name, avatar, standing and, for a member timed out, the end of the timeout, and for a member the name and color of their highest role (#323), 25 accounts a page, ranked by name unless the staff sort them by arrival or by highest role, among the accounts that the text searched and the filters of role, standing, arrival on the Discord server and age of the Discord account let through, the bot accounts left out unless the staff ask for them (#324). It keeps none of it, and the search runs on what it has just read, with no call to Discord's search. Reading every member needs the Server Members Intent, a privileged intent that Discord's Developer Portal lets the panel's two applications turn on, as they have since 2026-11-02 (#318). It lets the panel call List Guild Members, which requires it (https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/guild, read 2026-11-02), and the panel opens no connection to the gateway (ADR 0003), so it receives no event about any member.
- **The ticket archives on a record** (#197). For the staff who moderate, a record shows the support tickets its account opened: the Bot posts an archive in the archives channel when a ticket closes, with who opened it, the request kind, the dates it opened and closed, who closed it and the reason typed at the closing, and no conversation, which the Bot never reads (ADR 0009). At each view, the panel searches the channel's messages through Discord's search for the account's ID, which it sends to Discord, who holds it already, 100 results at most, newest first, keeps the archives that account opened and nothing afterwards, not even a cache, and reads the name of each closer from the Discord server. It writes nothing. An archive lasts as long as its message, in the channel, until a staff member deletes it, and deleting the message is how one is erased; neither the panel nor the Bot sets a duration (ADR 0009). Since #385, the page `/support-tickets` shows the archives of every account to every staff member, where only those who moderate saw them, on a record: the panel reads them as before, 25 a page, newest first, and keeps nothing of them either. Since #386, that page filters them: the panel sends Discord's search the text of one filter at a time, an account's ID, the words of a reason a staff member typed, or the emoji of a request kind, and checks the other filters in memory over the 100 messages it gets; for a name typed in place of an ID, it reads the Discord server's members and banned accounts, as `/records` does, to find the ID, and shows none of that list. It keeps none of it either.
- **The support tickets in progress on `/support-tickets`** (#388). At each view, for the whole staff, the panel reads the text channels of the tickets category (`TICKET_CATEGORY_ID`) whose name follows ShorBot's `{emoji}・{name}`, keeps those that the staff member views on Discord, worked out from their roles and the channel's permission overwrites (`src/staff/can-view-channel.ts`), and reads the last 100 messages of each. It shows the request kind, the opener's name, when the channel opened, taken from its ID, and when it was last active, the number of messages, the names of the staff members who wrote in it, the last message's author, an extract of its text of 160 characters at most, and a link to the channel on Discord. The conversation is made of what the opener and the staff wrote in a private channel, which the panel reads through Discord's REST API with the Message Content intent (ADR 0010). It keeps none of it, not even a cache, and writes nothing. The conversation lasts as long as the channel, which the Bot deletes 5 seconds after the ticket's closing (ADR 0009), leaving the archive.
- **The game log on `/game-log`** (#394). At each view, for the staff members who view the game log's channel on Discord (`GAME_LOG_CHANNEL_ID`), worked out as the tickets' are, the panel searches that channel's messages through Discord's search, 25 a page, newest first, for those whose author is the game server's webhook (`GAME_LOG_WEBHOOK_ID`), up to four searches a view, and shows for each line when it was posted, its category and action, the character it names and their game account, a number, the target, the reason and the place of the event, the line's identifier, and a link to the message on Discord, or, for a line out of format, its title and fields as the webhook wrote them. A character's name beside a game account's number can point to the player behind it, and a line names the staff member's command in game, so the game log counts as personal data (inferred). What a staff member types, a category, an action, a character, a game account, a target, a cell, a plugin, a reason, a word or a period, stays in the page's URL, `/game-log?category=…&action=…&character=…&account=…&target=…&cell=…&plugin=…&reason=…&text=…&from=…&to=…&before=…`, which the browser's history keeps too, and of it Discord's search gets one term, the game account's number, a word of the target, the character, the text or the reason, or the category's name, and the period's bounds; the panel checks every other filter itself on the messages the search gives (#395). The panel reads the content of messages another author posted with the Message Content intent (ADR 0010), keeps none of it, not even a cache, and writes nothing. A line lasts as long as its message in the channel, until a staff member deletes it (inferred; neither the panel nor the game server sets a duration).
- **The Bot log on `/bot-log`** (#396). At each view, for the staff members who view the Bot log's channel on Discord (`BOT_LOG_CHANNEL_ID`), worked out as the game log's is, the panel searches that channel's messages through Discord's search, 25 at a time, newest first, for those whose author is the application, up to four searches a view, and shows for each line when it was posted, its family and action, its fields, which name the accounts concerned, a role or a channel, and, for the closing of a support ticket, the reason that the member typed, and a link to the message on Discord, or, for a line out of format, its texts as the Bot wrote them. For each account a line names, the panel looks up its name on the Discord server, or on Discord for one that left, and keeps none of it. A line shows an account's name beside its ID, so the Bot log counts as personal data (inferred). What a staff member types, a word, a period, an account or a channel, goes to Discord's search, as the account's or the channel's ID once resolved, and the page's URL holds it, `/bot-log?family=…&action=…&account=…&channel=…&text=…&from=…&to=…&before=…`, which the browser's history keeps too. The panel reads the content of messages with the Message Content intent (ADR 0010), keeps none of it, not even a cache, and writes nothing. A line lasts as long as its message in the channel, until a staff member deletes it (inferred; neither the panel nor the Bot sets a duration).
- **What the panel writes into Discord, where Discord keeps it.** Every write's audit log reason names the staff member, as `Name (ID) : …` (ADR 0004), and Discord keeps audit log entries 45 days. A ban's reason, which the staff type, also goes into the Discord server's ban list, which `pnpm test:contract` read it back from (2026-10-29), and where it stays while the ban lasts (inferred, #181); a ban may also have Discord delete the account's messages of the last hour to the last 7 days, as the staff choose (#181). A timeout's end goes into the member's state on the Discord server, and its reason, which the staff type, into the audit log (#179). A kick's reason, which the staff type, goes into the audit log alone (#180). A sanction's direct message, which repeats both, stays in the DM channel between the panel's application and the member, which the application can delete, as the contract suite deletes its own (#179), and the member cannot (inferred). The panel keeps no copy of it.
- **What the logs and the alerts carry**: no member's ID, name or IP (`.claude/rules/monitoring.md`).
- **Who else touches it**: Vercel, which hosts the panel, keeps its runtime logs one hour (`CLAUDE.md`, **Log lines**), and receives each visitor's IP address, which the one rule of its Firewall counts requests by, to deny an address past 600 requests in 10 minutes (`CLAUDE.md`, **Rate limiting**), and Discord.

Discord's §5a already applies, so the panel owes a privacy policy without any store (inferred): `/privacy` publishes it since #186, to anyone, with or without a session, and the login pages and the navigation link it. It says in French what this section says, for « Shor Panel » and « Shor Panel Dev », keeps a placeholder for whoever is responsible and where to write to them, and names no legal basis, both of which wait on « Who is responsible for the panel's personal data, and who answers requests? » (`docs/agents/open-questions.md`). Its link goes into the Developer Portal of both applications by hand (#341). ShorBot, the Bot (ADR 0003), is another Discord application, which owes one too and has no public page, so `/privacy` becomes the one policy of Shor's Discord applications: #186 covers the panel, and ShorBot's part comes with its first feature in production, from the record of its processing that ShorBot keeps in its own repository (Gilles, 2026-10-28). This file stays the panel's.

## Planned

The store (#174) opens Neon for the warnings (#184), the staff's notes (#185) and the audit log beyond Discord's 45 days (#3). Before its first row, each point below holds, as the store's grilling decides (#175):

- **A legal basis**, likely legitimate interest, the GDPR's art. 6(1)(f), with its balance written down (inferred).
- **Information.** `/privacy` grows to say what the store keeps, why, for how long, who sees it, and how to ask for access, correction or deletion (§5a). A note the member never sees is data not collected from them, which the GDPR's art. 14 covers (inferred).
- **A retention period** for each datum, since art. 5(1)(e) keeps data no longer than its purpose needs, which the code enforces by deleting what is past it. The CNIL's three years after a member leaves an association is a reference point, not a rule for sanctions (inferred).
- **Deletion and correction on request**, which Discord's §5b exempts only for what a law requires to keep, weighed against keeping the trace of a ban (inferred).
- **Encryption at rest** (§5c): what Neon does there is unchecked.
- **The processor.** Neon's terms are the « Product Specific Schedule (Neon) », updated 2026-10-14, under Databricks' Master Cloud Services Agreement, which incorporates its DPA (https://neon.com/terms-of-service, https://www.databricks.com/legal/mcsa, updated 2026-02-20). No source names the Free plan.
- **The region.** Neon has Frankfurt, `aws-eu-central-1`, and no Paris, and "The region is fixed when the project is created" (https://neon.com/docs/introduction/regions). Vercel's `fra1` is Frankfurt too, and Hobby runs functions in a single region, `iad1` unless set (https://vercel.com/docs/functions/configuring-functions/region, updated 2026-08-11).
- **Backups.** Neon's Free plan keeps 6 hours of history and 1 manual snapshot, and "scheduled backups are not available on the Free plan" (https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans).
- **An impact assessment**, which the CNIL requires when a processing meets two of its nine criteria, among them « exclusion du bénéfice d'un droit/contrat » and « personnes vulnérables » (https://www.cnil.fr/fr/ce-quil-faut-savoir-sur-lanalyse-dimpact-relative-la-protection-des-donnees-aipd). Whether it applies waits on « Are there minors on Shor's Discord server? » (`docs/agents/open-questions.md`).
- **A record of processing**, of which this file is likely the draft (inferred), naming whoever is responsible, which waits on « Who is responsible for the panel's personal data, and who answers requests? » (`docs/agents/open-questions.md`).
- **Only what each feature needs**, as ADR 0004 already says. A support ticket's archive, which the panel reads from the archives channel rather than stores (ADR 0009), keeps who opened it, the kind of request, its dates, who closed it and the reason, but not the conversation (Gilles, 2026-10-28).
