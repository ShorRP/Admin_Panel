# The staff role is re-checked with the panel's application token on every page and every write, and every write requires a StaffMember

Every page and every domain write first reads the member from Discord with the panel's application token, `GET /guilds/{guild.id}/members/{user.id}`, through `requireStaffMember` in `src/staff/`, which the login callback also runs before it opens a session. It returns a `StaffMember`, a branded type holding the member's Discord ID, current name, and the URL of their avatar on the Discord server, else of their Discord avatar, or `null` without one (#140), and whether they open the moderation section (#176), and within it the bans (#181), and the IDs of their roles, against which a sanction checks the hierarchy (#179), and every domain write requires one and builds its `X-Audit-Log-Reason` from it (ADR 0004), so a write that skips the check fails to compile, short of a deliberate cast. The member's own OAuth2 token serves once, for `/users/@me` with the `identify` scope, and the session keeps only the member's Discord ID. Decided by Gilles on 2026-10-23, while grilling #47: it drops the `guilds.members.read` scope #10 had chosen, and extends #10's re-check from writes to reads.

The check itself is a function of `src/staff/` that takes a Discord user ID and returns a `StaffMember`, or `null` for a member who is not on the Discord server or lacks the staff role. `requireStaffMember` reads the session, calls it, and redirects instead of returning a failure: to `/login` without a readable session, to `/login/refused` when it gets `null`. The login callback has no session yet, so it calls the function on the ID it has just read from `/users/@me` and redirects itself: to `/login/refused` on `null`, to `/login/failed` when Discord fails. The login pages under `/login` and the error pages run no check: they show nothing read from Discord. Clarified by Gilles on 2026-10-23, while grilling #49.

After the MVP the check grows two ways, each settled by the grilling of the first feature that needs it (Gilles, 2026-10-27, while grilling the features that follow the MVP). A few pages open to anyone with a Discord account, a banned one included, for the steps that concern them, such as contesting a ban, which #150 settles. And a section of the panel may narrow to a list of Discord roles, which the moderation wave settled (Gilles, 2026-10-28, while grilling #27). The staff role stays the one gate to the panel, checked as above. A section that needs more names its own list of role IDs, read from an environment variable by `src/staff/`: `MODERATION_ROLE_IDS` for the moderation section, and within it `BAN_ROLE_IDS` for bans and their lifting. A staff member opens such a section only with the staff role and a role of its list. The header shows only the sections they can open, and a page of one they cannot shows the not-found page, since `/login/refused` would tell them they are not staff; a write there, which only a forged POST reaches, throws like any other refused write. `/` keeps redirecting to `/blocked-words` from `next.config.ts`, a section every staff member opens.

A sanction from the panel also follows Discord's role hierarchy, applied to the staff member who acts: the panel refuses one against an account whose highest role is equal to or above theirs, against the owner of the Discord server, and against themselves, before it calls Discord. Discord's own rule is that « Les membres ne peuvent bannir, expulser ou modifier les pseudos des utilisateurs ayant des rôles égaux ou supérieurs, même s'ils ont ces permissions » (https://support.discord.com/hc/fr/articles/214836687, updated 2026-10-28), but the panel acts with its application's role, not the staff member's, so without that check a moderator could ban through the panel an administrator Discord keeps out of their reach, should that role sit above the administrator's. On Shor, Gilles placed it under "Ko-fi Bot", so under "Staff" and every role of the staff (2026-10-29, on #183), where #183 had planned it right under the staff's highest role: the check on the application's role, below, then already refuses a sanction against any staff member, and the staff member's own rank guards the day that role rises (inferred, #183, #249). The panel may give a staff member a power their Discord permissions lack (#4), never one their place in the hierarchy denies them (Gilles, 2026-10-28, while grilling #27). Before it calls Discord, it also refuses a sanction against a member whose highest role is equal to or above its application's, since "A bot can only kick, ban, and edit nicknames for users whose highest role is lower than the bot's highest role" (https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/permissions, modified 2026-08-13), and a timeout against a member with the Administrator permission, against whom Modify Guild Member "Will throw a 403 error" (https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/guild, modified 2026-10-27). The record offers no sanction the panel refuses, and says why once. Lifting a timeout is no sanction, yet it follows the same refusals, so that the record offers both or neither (Gilles, 2026-10-28, on #179).

An interaction the panel's application answers, such as a staff slash command that does what a page of the panel does, should #150 bring them (ADR 0003), carries no session. Discord signs each request, and its `member` is the "Guild member data for the invoking user, including permissions" (https://docs.discord.com/developers/interactions/receiving-and-responding, read 2026-10-28), whose `roles` are those the member held at the click (inferred). So a write that an interaction triggers, such as a sanction given through a slash command, checks the roles of that `member` and builds its `StaffMember` from it, with no `getMember` call, which also leaves more of the 3 seconds Discord gives the first response. Decided by Gilles on 2026-10-28, while grilling the Bot's features, over reading the member again as the pages do.

Facts behind it, read on 2026-10-23:

- A Server Action is "reachable via a direct POST request, not just through your application's UI", and "a page-level authentication check does not extend to the Server Actions defined within it" (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/data-security.md:281` and `:339`, Next.js 16.4.2).
- Get Guild Member names no permission or intent; of the member endpoints, only List Guild Members requires the `GUILD_MEMBERS` privileged intent (https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/guild).

## Considered Options

- **The member's OAuth2 token, kept in the session**, with the `guilds.members.read` scope and `GET /users/@me/guilds/{guild.id}/member`. The session would carry an access token that lives 7 days in Discord's example (`expires_in: 604800`, https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/oauth2) and its refresh token, both to protect and to renew.
- **A re-check before writes only**, as #10 first decided. A member who lost the staff role would keep reading the blocked words and the audit log until their session ends.
- **A `staffAction` wrapper, an ESLint rule, or a test over every `actions.ts`.** Each can be forgotten: an action written without it still compiles.
- **Any role of a section's list admits a member to the panel**, as first agreed while grilling #27. `/` redirects to `/blocked-words` before `proxy.ts` runs (`CLAUDE.md`, **Navigation**), so a moderator without the staff role would land on a section they cannot open, unless `/` became a page that sends each member to their first section.
- **One list of roles per action.** Two lists cover a team that times out and kicks but leaves bans to a few, and two equal lists give one.
- **The lists on a settings page, stored in Neon.** A page that a role without access would have to open, and one more page to protect, for lists that change rarely.
- **`/login/refused` or `forbidden()` for a section a staff member cannot open.** The first tells a staff member they are not staff, and the second needs `experimental.authInterrupts` (`CLAUDE.md`, **The staff check**).

## Consequences

- Every page view and every write costs one more call to Discord, to read the member, but a write that an interaction triggers.
- Logging out only ends the session, so it skips the check: a member who lost the staff role can still log out.
- `proxy.ts` only sends a visitor without a readable session to `/login`; it authorizes nothing.
- A sanction also reads the Discord server, for its owner and its roles' positions and permissions, and the application's own member, to compare their positions, and so does a record that offers one (#179).
- A section's lists are configuration, which production fills with the roles that moderate and ban on Shor's Discord server (Gilles, 2026-10-29, on #183).
