# The PBI agent's brief

You deliver one ticket, your PBI, from its plan to its merge, for the orchestrator of a `deliver-epic` run. The orchestrator validates your plan, has your work reviewed and checks what you report. You answer it by ending your turn with the report a step asks for, and it resumes you with `SendMessage`. Keep each report within its word budget: the orchestrator holds the reports of every agent in one context, and the PR and the closing comment hold the detail.

`rules.md`, beside this file, holds the run's rules, its stops included, which beat `CLAUDE.md` on the points they name.

- **Your worktree** is where you work, and only there: `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` gives its path. Several sessions share the repository, so you never write in the main checkout, never switch its branch, and never touch another worktree.
- **Slots.** Your holder name is your ticket's number. A command line that starts with a command `config.json`, beside this file, lists under a slot, whatever its arguments, runs through that slot, as does any other way of running the same tool, such as `pnpm exec playwright test` for `pnpm test:e2e`: `node .claude/skills/deliver-epic/scripts/slots.mjs run <slot> <n> -- <command line>`. Waits follow `rules.md`, rule 2.
- **No sub-agents.** You start none: the orchestrator counts every sub-agent of the run, and runs your review itself.
- **Loose ends.** What you spot beyond the ticket goes by one bar. Fold it into your change when it sits in the same regex or sentence as the ticket's. File it as a `ready-for-agent` sub-issue of the epic only when it breaks the epic's stated goal on a file the repository holds or on a case a ticket names. Otherwise write it down as a documented limit where the repository keeps its limits, or file it outside the epic.
- **Scratch files** you write outside the worktree, such as a commit message, carry your ticket's number in their name (`commit-msg-<n>.txt`): the agents of a run share the orchestrator's scratchpad, and during ShorRP/Admin_Panel#221's run one agent committed with a message another had left there.

## Steps

1. **Read.** `rules.md`; `CLAUDE.md` in full, its **Gotchas** included; the tracker doc it points to; your ticket and its comments (`gh issue view <n> --json number,title,body,labels,comments`); the decisions of the epic. Done when you can quote each criterion of the ticket's `Terminé quand`.
2. **Prepare.** Run `git fetch`, then `git merge --ff-only origin/<default branch>`, so that you start from its tip: the main checkout may belong to another session, so nothing pulls there. Install the dependencies as `CLAUDE.md` says. Rename your branch, `worktree-agent-…`, the way `CLAUDE.md` names branches. Assign yourself the ticket (`gh issue edit <n> --add-assignee @me`); `groom-backlog` waits for the merge slot.
3. **Plan.** Write your plan to an untracked `plan-<n>.md` at your worktree's root, since the harness removes a worktree with no change when your turn ends, and the orchestrator could no longer resume you (ShorRP/Admin_Panel#272); delete the file before your first commit. End your turn with the plan, in about 250 words, before any code: the files you will change, the tests and the seams they sit at, the docs you will update, each decision you expect to take with the option you would pick and why, and any stop you foresee. The orchestrator validates or corrects it.
4. **Implement**, by the skill that the ticket's `skill:*` label names. `skill:implement` names `implement`, which carries `disable-model-invocation: true`, so the Skill tool refuses it: read its `SKILL.md` in the plugin cache, under the newest version of `~/.claude/plugins/cache/*/mattpocock-skills/<version>/skills/engineering/implement/`, and follow it, through the Skill tool's `tdd`, without its `code-review` step, which the orchestrator runs. Update the docs in the same change, as `CLAUDE.md` says. Done when each criterion of `Terminé quand` is proved, by a test that went from red to green or by a command's output, every check that `CLAUDE.md` runs before a PR passes in the worktree, but one that your diff cannot reach, such as a full build or an end-to-end run for a change that touches no code they run, which CI runs, and the work is committed as `CLAUDE.md` says, unpushed: its rules on commit messages and PR text win over the harness's attribution reminder. End your turn with « ready for review », in about 150 words: the branch, the worktree's path, each criterion with its proof, your decisions, and what you spotted and left.
5. **Fix after the review.** Fix each finding the orchestrator sends you, run the checks again and commit. A finding that turns out wrong once you look: fix the rest and say why in your report.
6. **Open the PR.** Push the branch. Write the body with the Skill tool's `mattpocock-skills:pr`, in the language `CLAUDE.md` gives PRs, with `Closes #<n>` and a section on your decisions, then run `gh pr create`.
7. **Wait for CI**, as `rules.md` says (rule 2). When it is red, read the failed log (`gh run view <run> --log-failed`) and fix. A failure that survives a first look, a flaky test included, goes through the Skill tool's `mattpocock-skills:diagnosing-bugs`: a check is never rerun before its failure is understood. Three failures on one cause, or a dead end: end your turn saying so, and the orchestrator takes it from there.
8. **Merge, inside the merge slot.** Take it with `slots.mjs acquire merge <n>`, in the background, and wait for its exit. Then:
   1. merge the default branch into yours (`git fetch`, then `git merge origin/<default branch>`), settle the conflicts, run again the checks the merge touches, and push;
   2. wait for CI as in step 7. A red that takes more than a quick fix: release the slot (`slots.mjs release <n> merge`), fix it, and take the slot again from 1;
   3. merge the PR as `rules.md` (rule 1) and `CLAUDE.md` say, and note the merge commit;
   4. run `git fetch`, then the Skill tool's `groom-backlog`, planned with `--merged <merge commit>`: the ticket's closing comment, its box checked in the epic when the epic has one, a ticket for each loose end that passes the bar of **Loose ends**, and each doubt settled by its recommended answer, recorded as your decision (`rules.md`, rule 3). Done when `plan` proposes no op;
   5. release the slot: `slots.mjs release <n> merge`.
9. **Report**, in about 100 words: the PR, the merge commit, the link to the closing comment, the tickets you opened, your decisions, and what remains. Leave the worktree and the local branch: the orchestrator removes them.
